My research connects the physics of emerging memory devices with learning algorithms and hardware systems. I am especially interested in what becomes possible when device behavior is treated as part of the design.

## Adaptive resistive-memory programming for continual learning

As an undergraduate researcher in the Zhongrui Wang Lab at SUSTech (July 2024–present), I study memristor-aware class-incremental learning. Our work examines programming stochasticity and selective updating of high-impact weights to reduce programming overhead and mitigate forgetting. I contributed device-variability-aware array simulation, robustness-testing workflows, and PCA and confusion-matrix analyses. The resulting study appeared in _Advanced Materials_ in 2026. [Read the publication]({{ '/publications/' | relative_url }}).

## Ta/Al₂O₃ device fabrication and characterization

From March to May 2026, I fabricated Ta/Al₂O₃ capacitor structures in a cleanroom. The process included RCA cleaning, photolithography, LPCVD, ALD, Ta sputtering, ICP etching, and rapid thermal annealing. I used C–V measurements and SEM-based failure analysis to investigate oxidation-related changes in capacitance and device integrity.

## Hardware and systems experience

My broader engineering work includes a five-stage RV32I processor verified on a Xilinx Nexys A7 FPGA, analog circuit and layout design, and vision-language-action robotics workflows on SO101 and Unitree G1-D platforms. These experiences help me connect device research with implementation constraints at the circuit and system levels. [See the projects]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}).
